# Game Design Document v0.1

## Core fantasy
Be a Scripture Explorer visiting illustrated biblical settings and discovering the narrative through observation, investigation, conversations, object interaction and environmental puzzles.

## Core loop
1. Arrive in a beautiful location, guided by cinematic camera and scene.
2. Receive an in-world mystery/quest.
3. Explore surroundings using keyboard/touch controls.
4. Investigate people and objects.
5. Solve a puzzle grounded in cited Scripture.
6. Witness the biblically established narrative outcome.
7. Unlock a character, story, place, and passage in the illustrated Living Journal.
8. Follow discovered connections to future stories.

## First adventure: David and Goliath
**Source:** 1 Samuel 17.
**Runtime target:** 10–15 minutes, adjustable after playtesting.
**Stages:**
- Valley arrival and exploration.
- Investigate Israelite army camp and the challenge.
- Meet characters through accurately sourced or clearly fictionalized dialogue.
- Discover David's background and motivations.
- Investigate Saul's armor and identify the five smooth stones.
- Scripted confrontation with player participation but canonical outcome.
- Journal evidence reconstruction and unlock related character cards.

## Educational goals
Players can state the main event sequence, identify David/Goliath/Saul/Eliab, find 1 Samuel 17, explain what the passage says about David's confidence, and distinguish biblical details from game additions.

## Mission mechanics
Use reusable `explore`, `inspect`, `talk`, `collect`, `sequence`, `connect`, `cinematic`, and `discover` objective types. Each objective has completion triggers, accessibility alternatives, prerequisites and source IDs.

## Narrative integrity
Players can make tactical choices, solve puzzles and select investigation order. Player actions never rewrite Scripture. Introduced characters/dialogues must be marked as narrative devices and should not imply an otherwise unsupported historical claim.

## Visual target
Cinematic cartoon 2.5D, warm layered village backgrounds, lively atmosphere, expressive characters; teen-friendly, not preschool or overly realistic. Prefer reusable animation states and environment tiles over AI-generated video.

## Player safety
No player-to-player messages in MVP; no unrestricted AI character roleplay; age-appropriate treatment of combat; no betting or lootboxes.

## Testable success
First-time player can complete the basic NPC conversation without instruction; most playtesters voluntarily explore; source links are visible in the journal; usability and fun are validated before content scaling.
